# Dead-Space Options (mock-up paths)

**Context:** Wall is **159¼″**. Three base units (27″ + 65″ + 27″ = **119″** carcasses). How we place them and treat leftover inches is the main open design fork.

**Locked (related):**
- **Risers:** 4″ on the **two side cabinets only**; center console stays at current height.
- **Countertop:** **Stepped** — side counters higher than the center top (~4″ before side-counter thickness).
- **TV:** Stays wall-mounted as today — no special surround / aperture / TV-field buildout.

Goal: tape and compare **Paths A / B / C**, pick one, then (if B) pick a/b/c before the plywood cut list.

**Shortlist:** Path **A** or **B.a** look best so far (photo mockups below).

**Visual mockups:**
- CAD: [mockups/](./mockups/) + canvas `entertainment-center-mockups.canvas.tsx`

**Beadboard (locked intent):**
- Path A — beadboard **only behind the unit** (not in open flank spaces)
- Path B.a — beadboard **wall-to-wall** on the upper backing (not on lower cabinet faces)

**Seating (locked intent):** shorter simple **white cushiony** chair (not the recliner), pulled forward in front of the left cabinets.

## Path A — Centered + open flanks

Keep the three-unit composite **centered** on the wall. Leave ~**14⅛″** open on each side. Block the outer ends of the built-in with trim; flanks stay painted wall (“open”).

| Topic | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Tape on wall** | Centerline; outer edges of 131″ (or actual composite width); mark 14⅛″ flanks L/R |
| **Where inches go** | Flanks stay empty; ~12″ inside the composite still needs a filler story between the three units (even gaps, or absorbed into stiles) |
| **Plywood impact** | Lowest flank material; mainly encasement + trim returns at ends |
| **Stepped countertop** | Side counters on the two raised cabinets only; center console top stays lower; flanks have no counter |

**Pros:** Least material; easy around doorway (left) and window/sofa (right).  
**Cons:** Less “wall-to-wall built-in”; side gaps can collect clutter.

---

## Path B — Cabinets to outer walls + gap treatment between

Push each **side cabinet out to the outer wall**. The large leftover space sits **between each side cabinet and the center console**. Treat those gaps with one of:

### B.a — Open shelving cubbies
Shallow open cubbies in the between-unit gaps (keep depth quieter than the 18″ center — e.g. ≤12–15″).

### B.b — Small cabinets
Closed boxes / small cabinet carcasses in the gaps (doors or false panels), painted White Dove to match.

### B.c — Other variation
Record during mock-up (e.g. mixed niche + closed, robot-vacuum bay, asymmetric L vs R). Leave as TBD until taped.

| Topic | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Tape on wall** | Side cabinets hard against L/R walls; mark gap width each side of the 65″ center; mock cubby/cabinet rectangles in those gaps |
| **Where inches go** | Flank clearance ≈ 0; gap budget ≈ (159¼ − 119) / 2 ≈ **20⅛″** each side of center (confirm on site) |
| **Plywood impact** | Extra boxes or shelves in two gaps; higher sheet count than A or C |
| **Stepped countertop** | Side counters can run wall → inner edge of side cabinet (or across cubby tops if B.a/B.b get a continuous side-bay counter); center stays lower |

**Pros:** True wall-to-wall presence; gaps become useful storage/display.  
**Cons:** More build and finish work; must respect doorway / sofa clearances at the outer ends.

---

## Path C — Even span + plywood mask

Distribute the three units so spacing is **even** from wall to wall and between center — then **mask all gaps with plywood fillers** (no open niches, no extra cabinets).

| Topic | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Tape on wall** | Equal (or near-equal) gaps: wall→side, side→center, center→side, side→wall; mark filler panel faces |
| **Where inches go** | (159¼ − 119) ≈ **40¼″** total distributed across four gap zones (or three if outer “gaps” are only scribe/trim — decide at tape-out) |
| **Plywood impact** | Moderate — filler skins / returns, not full cubby boxes |
| **Stepped countertop** | Side counters over each raised cabinet (+ optional filler tops if fillers are structural); center remains lower; fillers can carry face plane without matching center height |

**Pros:** Clean continuous White Dove plane; no clutter zones; simpler than B.  
**Cons:** No extra storage in gaps; large painted filler faces need good trim/edge detail.

---

## Mock-up steps (before choosing)

1. Painter’s-tape the wall for **Path A** (centered composite + 14⅛″ flanks). Live with it a day if you can.
2. Retape for **Path B** (cabinets to walls; mark between-unit gap widths). Optionally tape B.a vs B.b rectangles.
3. Retape for **Path C** (even gaps + filler faces).
4. For each path, mark **stepped height**: side cabinet top at ~34″ (4″ riser + ~30″ unit) vs center at ~30″.
5. Pick **Path A, B, or C**. If B, pick **a / b / c**.
6. Log the choice below, then update `working-plan.md` and close the matching items in `YOUR-TODOS.md`.
