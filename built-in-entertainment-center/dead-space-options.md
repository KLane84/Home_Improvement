# Dead-Space Options (to evaluate)

**Context:** Composite built-in is **131"** wide on a **159¼"** wall → **14⅛"** empty on **each** side. Also open: treatment of the center “TV field” between the two upper towers, and any filler gaps inside the 131" (carcasses sum to 119").

Goal: pick one primary strategy for the flanks (and optionally refine the center) before the plywood cut list.

---

## A. Side clearances (14⅛" left & right)

### Option A1 — Tall filler / return panels (simplest)
Plywood returns from the built-in’s outer edges back to the side walls (or a shallow vertical panel + scribe).  
**Pros:** Cheap, fast, clean White Dove plane.  
**Cons:** No storage; large blank walls unless broken with trim/shadow.

### Option A2 — Pull built-in wider with filler boxes
Add 14⅛"-ish closed plywood boxes (or shallower display niches) so the built-in reads wall-to-wall.  
**Pros:** True wall-to-wall built-in; can hide outlets / robot vacuum zone on one side.  
**Cons:** More plywood; must respect doorway (left) and window/sofa (right) clearances.

### Option A3 — Open display niches (floor to counter or full height)
Shallow open cubbies in the 14⅛" zones (12" deep max to stay quieter than the 18" center).  
**Pros:** Decorative storage; breaks up mass.  
**Cons:** Dust; more edge banding / finish work.

### Option A4 — Leave as painted wall + baseboard (intentional floating built-in)
Keep 14⅛" reveals; only scribe/trim the outer ends of the 131" unit.  
**Pros:** Least material; easier around doorway/window.  
**Cons:** Less “built-in to the architecture”; side gaps collect clutter (as in current photos).

### Option A5 — Asymmetric (different left vs right)
e.g. taller closed panel toward doorway, open niche or bench-height ledge toward sofa/window.  
**Pros:** Responds to real room use.  
**Cons:** Harder to keep balanced under White Dove “one composition” look.

---

## B. Center field (between upper towers / around TV)

### Option B1 — Flat panel surround (TV aperture only)
Plywood plane from countertop to crown with a cutout for the TV; no open shelves in the center.  
**Pros:** Calm, modern; hides cables easily.  
**Cons:** Large painted expanse; less display storage.

### Option B2 — Open shelves flanking TV (bridge or separate)
Shelves in the center bay beside/above TV, related to the 27" side towers.  
**Pros:** More storage/display; classic built-in library look.  
**Cons:** Harder cable concealment; shelf depths vs. TV thickness need care.

### Option B3 — Mantel / ledge under TV only
Deeper countertop or a secondary ledge under the TV, with closed panel above sides of TV.  
**Pros:** Place for soundbar / decor; clear hierarchy.  
**Cons:** Soundbar visibility becomes a design feature (good or bad).

### Option B4 — Picture-frame reveal
TV set into a shallow recess with a consistent reveal (½"–1") on all sides.  
**Pros:** Intentional, furniture-grade look.  
**Cons:** More precise carpentry; mount depth must be planned.

---

## C. Internal fillers (the ~12" inside the 131" envelope)

Carcasses = 119"; composite = 131" → ~12" to distribute as:

1. Equal gaps between the three units (e.g. ~6" each of two joints), skinned with plywood faces  
2. Wider center “TV plinth” alignment  
3. Absorbed into thicker outer stiles / face-frame look  

**Decision needed:** where those inches live before building the countertop (countertop wants a continuous substrate story).

---

## Recommendation for evaluation order

1. Tape the **131"** and **14⅛"** zones on the wall (painter’s tape) and live with it for a day.  
2. Choose **A** (flanks) first — it changes material count the most.  
3. Choose **B** (TV field) second — drives upper plywood cuts.  
4. Lock **C** (between-unit fillers) before ordering the countertop blank.
